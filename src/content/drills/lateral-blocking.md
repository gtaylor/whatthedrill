Wear full catcher's gear. Start with soft or tennis balls until the catcher's form is solid.

## How to run it

1. The catcher sets up in the secondary stance (runners-on stance). The partner kneels about 20 feet away with a pile of balls.
2. The partner points left or right, then throws a ball into the dirt about a step to that side.
3. The catcher pushes off the opposite foot, drops the knee on the ball side first, and gets the body in front of the ball. Glove goes down between the knees, chin tucked.
4. Angle the shoulders back toward home plate's center, so the ball bounces in front of the plate instead of off to the side.
5. Reset and go to the other side. Do 5 to each side, then mix them up without pointing first.

## Coaching cues

- "Move the body, not the glove."
- "Angle it back to center."
- "Chin down, glove closes the hole."

## Common mistakes

- Reaching with the glove instead of sliding the whole body over.
- Squaring up straight ahead, so the ball kicks off toward the dugout.
- Falling back on the heels, which lets the ball bounce away.

## Make it easier or harder

- **Easier:** set balls on the ground left and right and move to block them at walking speed, like the Three-Ball Blocking drill.
- **Harder:** the partner throws from pitching distance, mixes in strikes the catcher has to catch, and adds balls farther outside.
