Safety first: helmets on, feet-first slides only (no headfirst for young players), sneakers or socks on the tarp, and check the grass underneath for rocks and sticks.

## How to run it

1. Lay a slip-and-slide or tarp on flat grass and wet it down. Put a throw-down base at the far end.
2. Start sitting on the tarp. Bend one leg and tuck it under the other knee so your legs make the shape of a "4." The top leg points at the base with a slight bend in the knee.
3. Lean back a little and put your hands up by your chest or ears. Practice getting into the "4" quickly, both legs, until it feels natural.
4. Next, start from kneeling. Fall back gently into the "4" and slide a few feet. Then try it from a walk, then a jog.
5. When players are ready, have them run and start the slide about two body lengths before the base. Sit down into it. Don't jump.
6. Slide into the base with the top foot and let the bent leg and backside take the ground.

## Coaching cues

- "Make a 4."
- "Sit down, don't jump."
- "Hands up."

## Common mistakes

- Jumping or leaping into the slide instead of dropping smoothly to the ground.
- Putting hands down to catch the fall, which can jam fingers or wrists.
- Starting the slide too late and jamming the ankle into the base.

## Make it easier or harder

- **Easier:** no water. Slide on a big piece of cardboard on grass, or on wet grass with sneakers.
- **Harder:** move to a dirt base path in sliding shorts, and have a coach call "slide" or "stand up" at the last second.
