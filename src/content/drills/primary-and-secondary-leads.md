Many leagues for players 12 and under don't allow leads until the pitch reaches the batter. Use this drill only if your league allows leadoffs.

## How to run it

1. Runners line up behind first base with helmets on. A coach stands on the mound as the pitcher. A player with a glove plays first base.
2. The first runner steps on the bag, finds the pitcher, and walks off with small side shuffles. Never cross the feet. Stop at the same spot every time, about one big step and a dive away.
3. Set the lead stance: feet a little wider than the shoulders, knees bent, weight on the balls of the feet, hands loose in front.
4. If the coach throws over to first, the runner gets back with a crossover step or dives back to the outside corner of the bag.
5. If the coach goes home, the runner takes two hard shuffles toward second. The right foot should land just as the pitch reaches the plate. That's the secondary lead.
6. From there, the runner either sprints to second or hurries back, based on the coach's call. Then the next runner goes.

## Coaching cues

- "Same lead every time."
- "Eyes on the pitcher."
- "Right foot lands as the ball gets there."

## Common mistakes

- Taking a different size lead every time, which tips off the pitcher.
- Crossing the feet while shuffling and getting caught off balance.
- Standing tall and flat-footed instead of staying low and ready.

## Make it easier or harder

- **Easier:** no throw-overs at first. Just practice walking off to the same spot and doing the secondary shuffles.
- **Harder:** the coach mixes in throw-overs at random, and runners take their secondary lead off a real pitch to a catcher.
