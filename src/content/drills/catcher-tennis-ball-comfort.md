The catcher wears all the gear, including the mask, helmet and protective cup, every time.

## How to run it

1. The catcher gears up and gets in a comfortable squat. The tosser kneels 15 feet away.
2. Gently toss a few tennis balls off the mask and the chest protector. Ask the catcher what they felt. Usually it's "nothing."
3. Now the catcher catches tennis balls with the bare throwing hand, keeping the mitt hand behind the back. The job is to keep the eyes open.
4. Put the mitt on. Toss tennis balls to different spots and have the catcher catch and hold each one still for a second.
5. Finish with 5 tosses in the dirt. The catcher just drops to the knees and lets the ball hit the chest protector.

## Coaching cues

- "Eyes open, watch it hit the glove."
- "The gear has you. Trust it."
- "Throwing hand behind your back."

## Common mistakes

- Turning the head or closing the eyes when the ball comes in.
- Leaving the throwing hand out in front, where foul tips can hurt it.
- Tossing hard or too soon. Speed comes only after the catcher stops flinching.

## Make it easier or harder

- **Easier:** stand closer and toss slower, or start with the catcher kneeling instead of squatting.
- **Harder:** toss from farther away and firmer, then switch to soft baseballs or a pitching machine set slow.
