Warm up first and stop if anything hurts. Pitchers: long toss counts as throwing, so follow pitch-count and rest guidelines such as MLB and USA Baseball's Pitch Smart.

## How to run it

1. After your normal throwing warm-up, start 30 to 45 feet apart with easy throws.
2. Every 4 or 5 throws, both partners take a few steps back.
3. As the distance grows, throw with a smooth arc and a crow hop. Throw with intent, but stay relaxed.
4. Stop backing up when you can't reach your partner in a few hops with good form. As a guide, that's about 90 to 120 feet for most 11- and 12-year-olds and 120 to 150 feet for 13- and 14-year-olds. Older players go farther only after building up over several weeks.
5. Work back in. Shorten the distance step by step, throwing firmer on a line, and finish with a few easy throws at about 60 feet.

## Coaching cues

- "Arc it, don't muscle it."
- "Crow hop into every long throw."
- "Same arm path near and far."

## Common mistakes

- Going too far too soon, especially early in the season.
- Leaning way back and heaving the ball with a different arm path.
- Long tossing when the arm is tired or right after a heavy pitching day.

## Make it easier or harder

- **Easier:** stop at a shorter distance and make fewer throws.
- **Harder:** add distance over weeks, not days, and add more firm throws on a line coming back in.
